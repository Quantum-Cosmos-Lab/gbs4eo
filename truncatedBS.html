Overlap across all angles

The vertical line marks the current θ. For $n\le3$ the curve stays at 1: these photon-number blocks fit inside the cutoff, so the truncated generator is exact.

The circuit amplitudes are computed by simulating the 28 two-qubit-gate circuit gate by gate. The ideal beam splitter is $e^{\theta(\hat a^\dagger\hat b-\hat a\hat b^\dagger)}$ in the full photon-number block, with no cutoff.

The overlap ignores the overall sign of each output state, which the amplitude view shows. At θ = π/2, for example, the circuit sends |3,3⟩ to +|3,3⟩, while the ideal beam splitter gives −|3,3⟩.

Photon number is conserved, so an input with $n=n_1+n_2$ photons only reaches the states $|k,n-k\rangle$. With two qubits per mode, the register holds $k\le3$ and $n-k\le3$. For $n\ge4$ the ideal beam splitter sends light to states the register can’t represent, and the truncated generator rotates the remaining states at different rates, $2\sqrt3\,\theta$ instead of $2\theta$ for $n=4$.
